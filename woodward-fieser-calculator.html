1 What Are the Woodward–Fieser Rules?

For a conjugated diene, the Woodward–Fieser rules let you build up the expected λmax of the π→π* transition from a fixed base value, by adding a small increment for every structural feature attached to the chromophore.

  • Start from a base value that depends on whether the diene is acyclic / heteroannular (the two double bonds are in different rings, or one/both are open-chain) or homoannular (both double bonds sit inside the same ring, in an s-cis arrangement).
  • Add +30 nm for every carbon–carbon double bond that extends the conjugation beyond the parent diene.
  • Add +5 nm for every alkyl group or ring-residue directly attached to one of the four diene carbons.
  • Add +5 nm for every double bond that is exocyclic to a ring (one of its carbons is in the ring, the other is outside it).
  • Add +39 nm once if the system additionally contains a homoannular diene component fused to the main chromophore.
  • Certain auxochromes (–OR, –SR, –Cl/–Br, –NR₂, –OCOCH₃) attached to the diene add their own fixed increment.

Sum every increment onto the base value — that sum is your predicted λmax, in nm.
